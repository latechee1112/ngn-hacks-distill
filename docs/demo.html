City Council Approves New Bike Lane Network Downtown

By Jordan Cassidy · Staff Reporter · Updated 2 hours ago

The city council voted 6–1 on Monday night to approve a long-debated plan that will add 4.3 miles of protected bike lanes to the downtown core, connecting the river district to the university corridor for the first time with a continuous, physically separated route.

Supporters say the network will cut commute times for the roughly 3,000 residents who already bike to work downtown, while easing pressure on a bus system that has struggled with overcrowding during peak hours. Opponents, largely business owners along Fifth Street, argued the lanes would eliminate nearly 80 parking spaces along the route.

"We heard the concerns loud and clear, and this final version protects about half of those spaces by shifting some lanes to side streets," said Council Member Priya Nadella, who sponsored the amended proposal. Construction is expected to begin in October and wrap before the winter freeze.

The project is funded through a combination of a federal active-transportation grant and the city's existing capital improvement budget, with no new tax increase required, according to city finance officials who presented alongside the transportation department at Monday's meeting.
